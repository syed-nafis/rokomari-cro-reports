Buy-now is performing. It pulls 1.6× more entries than regular checkout and still lands ~90% of its purchases (7,241 vs 8,020) — while also serving as a total-cost preview. The 22% completion is the expected signature of that dual use, not a broken funnel.
Buy-now captures more intent. 32,609 users click it — 3.05% of visitors, 1.6× the 20,038 who enter regular checkout. The express CTA clearly pulls people in.
Lower completion is expected, not lost intent. Many users tap buy-now to preview their total cost (with delivery) before deciding — so a good share of the 22.2% completion reflects price discovery. Even so, buy-now nets ~90% of regular checkout's purchases (7,241 vs 8,020) while serving that need.
Refinement: surface total cost earlier. Showing delivery + final price on the product/cart — before buy-now — means price-checkers don't need to enter the flow just to see it, converting more of those clicks into completed orders without hurting the preview use.

Key learnings
Checkout-level tests delivered the biggest revenue this month
The Order Summary dropdown redesign (Test 3, Variant C) lifted purchase rate 67.2% → 68.6% on high-intent checkout traffic and projects to ৳1,67,890/month — the single largest win of the month. Optimising the bottom of the funnel, where users already intend to buy, returned more per visitor than any top-of-funnel acquisition tweak. Prioritise checkout and cart surfaces.
Device type can flip the winner — never ship on a blended result
The Superstore title test was flat-to-negative on the full funnel (Bangla −0.7pp purchase) yet won decisively on mobile alone — Bangla +3.6pp purchase (7.6% vs 4.0%) and +7.4pp ATC. A single pooled number completely masked opposite behaviour across devices. Device-segmented analysis must become standard before every ship decision — this is the strongest signal of the month.
Language preference is contextual, not universal
English won the cart ATC-interaction test (+7.2pp purchase, 12.3% → 19.5%) while Bangla won on the Superstore mobile segment. There is no fixed "best language" — it shifts by surface and by device. Bangla vs English should be A/B'd per placement rather than decided once and rolled out everywhere.
Intent-driven copy is the cheapest high-leverage lever
The featured-category title test (Variant C) lifted click-through from 3.1% → 17.9% — a ×5.8 jump — with no design change at all. Copy that signals clear value intent outperformed every design-only test this month and ships fast. Build a copy-testing backlog for high-traffic entry points.
Low purchase counts keep small-traffic tests directional only
Several experiments ran at ~1–2K participants/arm with too few purchases to reach statistical significance (Superstore, Boimela, Original-swap) — their lifts are directional, not confirmed. Validate promising winners by scaling them onto high-traffic surfaces before treating the revenue as banked.
Short runtimes need a high-traffic caveat
Our biggest win (Order Summary, 4 days) reached volume fast because checkout traffic is dense, but shorter windows miss weekly cycles on lower-traffic surfaces. Hold low-traffic tests to a ≥7-day window with a pre-set GrowthBook sample-size target, and confirm short high-traffic wins over a full week before a 100% rollout.
Next frontier: segment by device and by city
This month's device split proved that audience cohorts respond in opposite directions to the same change. The same is almost certainly true geographically — Bangladesh's major cities (Dhaka, Chattogram, Sylhet, Khulna) differ in delivery speed, COD-vs-online payment mix and price sensitivity, none of which we have tested yet. June's roadmap formally adds device-type and city-level experiment cohorts to capture these differences and stop averaging away real wins.
