1. Order-track — "Go to Cart" after Add-to-Cart
On the order-track page, after adding an item the button stays a dead "Added to cart" state. Changing it to "Go to Cart" — so the next click navigates straight to the cart — gives users a direct path onward and should lift view-cart, checkout and purchase rates. Same interaction pattern that won on the Superstore page in May.

Key learnings
The Go-to-Cart interaction pattern is now proven twice
The order-track test lifted purchase +1.14pp across every funnel stage and both devices — the same pattern that won on the Superstore page in May (+59% purchase). Dead-end "Added to cart" states cost real orders; making them navigable is the clearest repeatable win we have. Roll the pattern to every ATC surface.
Urgency moves add-to-cart, not purchases
The PDP low-stock label lifted ATC +2.4pp but purchases stayed flat, and the cart quantity-left variant trended negative (−1.5pp purchase). Scarcity gets items into carts but doesn’t close them — and inside the cart it may even read as pressure. Use scarcity at the browse stage, not at the decision stage.
Trust badges are not a free lift
The cart trust-badge test lost on solid traffic (−0.57pp purchase, ~5K/arm). Reassurance content placed directly above the CTA appears to add clutter at the decision moment rather than confidence. Placement and weight matter more than the message.
Less copy is safe — keep streamlining checkout
Removing the payment-section title and subtitle was conversion-neutral on ~3.9K/arm. Redundant copy can be stripped without risk, which validates the wider checkout copy-streamlining track (TEST-057).
Device split flipped the OTP login read — again
Blended, OTP-first login is flat; split by device, desktop drops sharply (login −4.1pp, purchase −0.86pp) while mobile improves (+0.26pp purchase). Exactly like May’s Superstore finding, the pooled number masks opposite behaviour — device-segmented analysis stays mandatory before any ship call.
Niche-category tests are traffic-starved
The Islamic-section and budget-books tests ran at 37–41 users/arm — the Bangla title’s +19pp ATC is exciting but unprovable at this volume. Low-traffic surfaces need multi-week windows, or the winning patterns should be re-tested on high-traffic sections instead.
